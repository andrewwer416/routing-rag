Football is a family of team sports involving kicking a ball to score. Association football, rugby football, American football, Australian rules football and Gaelic football are different codes.

Association football is played by two teams of eleven players. FIFA is the international governing body of association football. Players generally use their feet; the goalkeeper may handle the ball within the penalty area. The FIFA World Cup is an international association football competition.

Rugby football developed in English schools. Rugby union and rugby league allow carrying the ball and scoring tries. American football developed from rugby football and introduced distinctive rules including the forward pass. These connections reflect a shared history, while each code has its own rules.

Football codes differ in how players handle the ball, how points are scored and how play restarts. Association football emphasizes kicking, while rugby and American football combine carrying and kicking. The evolution of school rules contributed to the formation of different football codes.

The governance of football depends on the code being played. FIFA governs association football internationally, while rugby union and rugby league have separate governing organizations. A governing organization can coordinate international competition, publish rules and work with national associations. Local clubs and school teams participate within these broader structures. The word football alone does not identify a single set of rules or a single governing organization. A reader asking about a governing body should specify the code. An international association football competition and an international rugby competition therefore belong to different sporting structures even when both are described as football.

The movement of the ball illustrates the differences between codes. In association football, an outfield player generally moves the ball by kicking it or using another permitted part of the body rather than the hands. In rugby, players can carry the ball and kick it, but passing and contact follow the rules of that code. American football also allows carrying and kicking, and the forward pass is a distinctive part of its play. These descriptions provide a broad comparison rather than a complete rulebook. Details of penalties, player positions and scoring require the rulebook for the particular competition.

Shared history connects several football codes without making their modern rules identical. School traditions helped shape different approaches to carrying, kicking and physical contact. Rugby football is connected historically to American football, which developed additional rules and a distinctive form of play. Association football followed a separate codification of its rules. Comparing the codes can therefore involve both historical connections and present-day differences.
